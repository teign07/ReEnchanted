# Chapter 6 science audit and applied upgrades

Applied October 23, 2026, to the bundled Wonder Compass in `Shared/BookReferenceLibrary.json`.

Scope: Chapter 6A, Chapter 6B, and only the Chapter 6 subsection of Notes & References. Research was checked before edits. No build, device installation, deployment, or commit.

## Editorial result

The chapter still asks the reader to find a Spark, feel the Tug, and go investigate. Its tools and stories remain. The main repair is the explanation of attention: a useful filter metaphor no longer carries unsupported claims about a single programmable brainstem structure, deleted percentages of reality, overheating, or permanent neural recalibration.

The additions connect studies to things a reader can try: describing a few visible details before inventing a story, letting a sad song or gray sky be company, quieting notifications, and creating a moment to choose before entering a feed. The technical qualifications and source details live in the actual book notes.

### The 28% source trail remains unresolved

The original book's Chapter 4 attributed the figure to a 2021 thirty-day curiosity intervention. Its notes cited “The Five-Dimensional Curiosity Scale Revised (5DCR): Valid work outcomes.” Chapter 6 repeated the figure while referring to the Curiosity and Exploration Inventory.

The real [5DCR paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2020.109836) was published in 2020 and develops a curiosity measure using two samples and survey follow-ups. Kashdan's own publication list places this paper and a separate workplace-curiosity paper under a 2021 heading marked “in press,” so the date discrepancy alone does not invalidate the original author's source trail. The full 5DCR paper and the earlier CEI-II paper were inspected; they do not report the described thirty-day intervention.

The full [2018 five-dimensional curiosity paper](https://www.daviddisabato.com/s/Kashdan-et-al-2018-five-dimensional-curiosity-scale.pdf) reports two profile groups each comprising approximately 28% of its sample. This is a concrete occurrence of the number in the relevant research family. It has not been established as the source of the book's well-being increase. Correlations, sample proportions, explained variance, and percentage improvements must not be substituted for one another.

Status: exact claimed well-being increase **not yet traced**, rather than proved false or invented. The valid relationship between curiosity and well-being remains in the chapter. The precise 28% should be restored with its correct meaning if a matching source is found. This unresolved editorial trail belongs in this research record, not in reader-facing prose.
