# Chapter 8B: science review and applied changes

Reviewed and applied October 22, 2026. Scope: the bundled **Chapter 8B: Characters**, its retrieval prompt and preview, and its subsection of **Notes & References** in `Shared/BookReferenceLibrary.json`.

## Editorial judgment

The chapter has a defensible field-guide invitation: deliberately giving familiar things a character can offer another way to notice, care, play, and begin a task. There is direct research on several ingredients and applications. There is no basis for presenting every proposed benefit, the six-step exercise, or the Compass system as experimentally established.

The manuscript's largest problems were stronger-than-measured outcomes, reversed causal directions, mistaken bibliographic identities, and brain/hormone explanations that exceeded the evidence. Repairing those leaves the central invitation intact. The practical instruction remains direct; distinctions about study design and generalization live mainly in the notes.

The complete chapter and its original notes were read, including the mission examples, full Compass cycle, conclusion, and summary. Research included original papers, publisher abstracts, author-hosted manuscripts, institutional publication records, and recent work available through October 22, 2026. Some publisher full texts were unavailable; conclusions from those sources were restricted to their accessible abstracts and bibliographic records. This was a targeted research audit, not a systematic review of the entire literature.

## Research that strengthens the chapter

- **Actual care-related behavior:** [Chan (2021), Saving Mr. Water](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resconrec.2021.105814). A randomized household field trial compared water-saving newsletters with and without anthropomorphic framing and measured lower water use in the anthropomorphic condition. Added a short passage under the utilitarian-trance discussion. This is stronger support for a specific care-to-action link than the former maintenance and plant-survival assertions.
- **Connection to nature:** [Tam, Lee, and Chao (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2013.02.001). Supports felt connection and conservation-related intentions. Added to the notes with the distinction between intentions and Chan's measured water use.
- **Keeping existing possessions:** [Chandler and Schwarz (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2009.12.008). Moved the real replacement-intention result into the Object Loyalty argument, where it belongs.
- **Self-explanation:** [Bisra and colleagues (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9434-x). Supports the broader explanatory practice behind the rubber duck, without inventing a duck-specific mechanism.
- **Humorous reframing:** [Samson and Gross (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1080/02699931.2011.585069). Supports the sitcom frame as an emotion-regulation application, without a printer-specific cortisol promise.
- **Gratitude:** [Choi and colleagues (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2425193122). Supports small average well-being gains across 145 papers. Added to the gratitude notes and qualified in the existing practice passage.
- **Individual differences:** [Folk, Heine, and Dunn (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-19212-2). Supports immediate connection after a brief chatbot conversation and variation related to anthropomorphism. It strengthens the existing optional approach, rather than a claim that everyone should feel the same thing.

## Current findings that constrain the argument

- [Durand and Bègue (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actpsy.2026.107436): no significant overall relationship between chronic loneliness and anthropomorphism. This synthesis addresses an association; it neither proves nor disproves an untested Compass intervention.
- [Deng, Chang, and Geng (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2026.116272): in service-robot studies, human-like presentation can raise unmet expectations and increase loneliness in temporarily lonely consumers. This is a specific context, not a reason to remove imaginative play with a mug.
- [Liu and Li (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jretconser.2024.104019) and [Ishii, Choi, and Oishi (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cresp.2026.100289): disposal guilt, retaining idle products, and associations with hoarding support a meaningful stop condition. The association is not an inevitable consequence of playful personification.
- [Hortensius and colleagues (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.25542): challenges a simple identity between anthropomorphism and general mentalizing ability.
