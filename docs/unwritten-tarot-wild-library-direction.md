# The Unwritten Deck: the wild library

October 27, 2026. This reader-directed world supersedes the Greek/Mediterranean
visual treatment in the first two production passes. Their useful bookcraft
ideas, traditional card identities and count checks survive. Their world and
casting are not the approved direction. The v2 pass is preserved and barred
from installation; it was never installed.

## The world

A seemingly endless, mostly wild library Academy: crooked timber stairs,
rambling rooms, root-bound shelves, impossible landings, lamps, worn bindings,
faerie bargains and books with habits. Ink and transparent watercolor on handled
paper remain the medium. The reader named magical-academy fiction, *Labyrinth*
and the neverending library as inspirations; these inform ReEnchanted's own
architecture and atmosphere, rather than reproducing a film set.

Classical robes, marble colonnades and Mediterranean landscapes were the wrong
default. Use the existing Cast illustrations, Book Fae dossiers and actual
library artwork as primary references. Historical Rider–Waite–Smith cards supply
gesture, relationships and symbols, not the replacement world's wardrobe.

All current Cast members may appear. Older Enchantify Academy characters with
authored lore are also available. Casting should follow the card and preserve
established identities; an allegorical tarot appearance does not silently add
new powers or story facts. Do not cast all 78 before the previews are discussed.

Literary elves of both Seelie and Unseelie courts, Deep Lore Dwarves, marginalia
goblins, punctuation pixies and other fae belong here. Give them occupations,
manners and relationships to books. Avoid reducing the courts to a good/evil
color code, dwarves to comedy miners, or every fae to the same pointed-ear human.

## Approved direction and refinements

The reader approved the direction of the four previews, then refined two
points: vary the rooms and increase the marginalia. Books remain everywhere,
including the Kitchens. These refinements govern the next cards.

After seeing the classroom, Great Hall, common-room and richer Penny margin
studies, the reader said, "I like these directions." Treat the varied rooms,
ubiquitous books and fuller authored marginalia as the accepted direction for
subsequent production. This does not remove the classroom's documented detail
corrections or replace per-card symbol and count review.

The reader explicitly reaffirmed that every card stays grounded in the
Rider–Waite–Smith set, and authorized unattended continuation overnight on
October 27. Preserve all 78 identities, the corresponding RWS tableau and
essential symbols, relationships and gestures, and exact numbered suit counts.
ReEnchanted casting, rooms, bookcraft and fae reinterpret that structure; they
do not replace it with unrelated fantasy illustrations. The next phase may
continue beyond previews, with per-card review before replacement. The standing
instruction remains: never build unless the reader asks.

## Rooms throughout the Academy

The wild Stacks are one location in the Academy. Use ordinary reading rooms,
classrooms, the Great Hall, Chapter common rooms, workshops, faculty spaces,
the Kitchens, conservatories and quieter libraries as the card requires. A
recognizable room with a few particular enchantments can carry the world.
Reserve immense impossible shelf architecture for cards where it earns its
place. Vary daylight, lamplight, season, scale, company and degrees of disorder.

Every setting remains full of books or booklike things. Common rooms have
borrowed volumes on chairs, shared stories and shelf corners. Classrooms have
lesson folios, practice bindings and books in use. The Great Hall has communal
reading tables and volumes carried between people. Kitchens have flour-dusted
cookbooks, recipe leaves, domestic annotations, pantry shelves of books and
volumes propping up bowls. A quieter room does not become a generic interior.

Chapter common-room staging should follow each Chapter's existing philosophy
and character; these illustrations do not silently establish new lore.

## More inhabited margins

Keep it present and purposeful: a named material or specimen, a small related
fae action, a diagram or repair, and a short reading observation. It should feel
like someone has lived with this particular card. Leave breathing room and
preserve the main action at thumbnail size. Never obscure a counted object.

The first four previews were too sparse in their margins. Build a few connected
marks around each next card: a named specimen or material, a second small study
or diagram, a tiny fae action, a short aside/correction/disagreement, and the
reading note. Vary this vocabulary rather than stamping the same four objects
onto every card. Botanical labels name what is actually shown. Keep all prose
authored and short; annotations should not become a surrounding worksheet.

Exact labels and reading notes remain authored live handwriting, with an
accessible transcript. Generated illustration supplies studies and small
narrative marks. No imitation prose. Physical punctuation glyphs may appear
as fae objects; they are not generated sentences.

## October 29 refinement: margins belong to the incident

The reader asked to vary marginalia placements so the deck does not look
templated. Studies may sit low, run down an edge or share space with an
object; small fae can inhabit the scene. Exact authored notes can appear
beside the detail they notice, with a restrained leader when useful.
Keep essential symbols, counted suit objects, faces and hands unobscured.
Do not rotate the same corner stickers or impose a new universal pattern.
Preserve successful existing paintings; edit only the cards that benefit.
The Book's voice remains feral and child-like in its physical attention,
without baby talk, generic counselling or a compulsory lesson on each card.


The second October 29 pass extends scene-specific placement across six further
paintings and twenty native-only cards. Quiet upper notes, low studies, narrow
edge specimens, and small helpers inside rooms are all available. Placement
must be checked against the finished painting: never obscure a face, hand,
counted symbol, or another note just to achieve variety. Preserve the wording
of successful notes. Every additional pass ends with a complete-deck audit.
