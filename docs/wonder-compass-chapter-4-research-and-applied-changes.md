## Current and additional research surveyed

The survey included original studies, syntheses and null results, with coverage through 22 October 2026. It is targeted rather than systematic.

- [Vagal recovery during VR awe (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mhp.2026.200529) and [Takano & Nomura (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-49681-2) appear in the notes. They strengthen the rationale for studying intensity and calm together without turning a stress-recovery sequence into a controlled demonstration of an awe-specific vagus switch.
- [Banakou et al. (2026), Counterclockwise VR](https://doi.org/10.2196/88338), appears in the notes as a recent, preliminary analog. Its 23-person younger-self/current-self comparison is relevant, but the small sample and short follow-up do not warrant a second main-text aging promise. Its account also supports retaining the original retreat's luggage anecdote in attributed form.
- [Graziosi et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0356728) appears in the notes with the earlier positive work. Its replication failures concern selected laboratory/online inductions and outcomes; it did not directly repeat the outdoor eucalyptus experiment. The main text attributes the time-availability finding rather than treating it as universal.
- [Eilert et al. (12 October 2026)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1965838) tested daily reflection aimed at several emotional resources in 44 students. The authors reported increased flexibility mindset relative to an active control, but distress improved in both conditions; targeted emotions and adherence were not directly measured, tasks differed in demands, and a relevant commercial interest was disclosed. Not added to the book: the study cannot isolate awe, and the existing evidence fits the chapter more directly.
- [Bogaert et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1111/aphw.12578) compared high- and low-awe nature clips. Improvements in both groups make it useful context for ordinary nature's value, but not evidence that only intense awe works. Not added as another body paragraph: the chapter already has a direct everyday-nature invitation, and the acceptance paragraph protects the reader from demanding a spectacular payoff.
- Expressive-writing research was checked against [Reinhold et al.'s original review](https://doi.org/10.1111/cpsp.12224). The body now relies on the earlier chapters' savoring and memory-cue research for keeping moments; it does not borrow a depression-treatment percentage from a different writing activity.
