# First Pages: copy review

Updated 12 October 2026. This describes the source changes. It is not a record of a build or a device walkthrough.

The Book starts with things a reader knows: their mug, a sound in the kitchen, a reading snack, a recent memory. The unexpected part is what happens to those things in the story. Questions say what to answer. Controls say what they do. The Book writes, Zara speaks, and Wicker interrupts; changes of speaker are named.

## The reading sequence

The existing nine opening beats and fifteen flow steps remain in place. Saved-answer keys and choice identifiers are preserved.

| Opening beat | What the reader understands and does |
| --- | --- |
| Find the Book | A loose Page falls out. The Book is writing on it and wants to talk. |
| Changing ink | A word disappears. Tap whether you spotted it. The Book tells you which word it removed. |
| An ordinary memory | Choose a recent familiar drive, walk, or time making food. For each of three details, tap whether you remember it. No typed answer is required. |
| A vivid memory | Choose what you were doing in a recent memorable moment. Write one detail, with a plain example. |
| The Curse | The Book compares the answers without claiming they diagnose the reader. Three research examples explain attention and routine. |
| How the Book helps | The Book offers questions and small adventures. Choose what makes your days blur. |
| First sentence | Look up from the screen and write one thing you actually notice. Examples can be changed to fit. Choose Keep or Let it wait. |
| Two answers | Say what usually happens to moments you want to remember, and whether writing this one down helped you notice something new. |
| Story style | Choose Small and Everyday, A Little Adventure, or Surprise. Your actual words stay separate from the fiction around them. Open the Book to follow the Page. |

| Inside the Book | What happens next |
| --- | --- |
| Arrival | You fall between lines of ink and land in the Academy library. Zara introduces herself and helps you stand. |
| Snack | Zara asks what you eat while reading. “Nothing” is an answer too. |
| Meet the others | Three named characters disagree about your snack. |
| Name | Write a first name, nickname, or name for this story. |
| Belief | Write something you believe, with ordinary examples. Belief points and adding an idea to the Cast are explained separately. |
| Inkbones | Tap for a practice roll. A later roll will settle the scene with Wicker. |
| What you enjoy | Choose what makes a day feel special, then what you usually do when you notice something good. |
| Pagewright | Open the full studio to arrange words, photos, paper, and marks. Export → Keep in Book saves the image for the first edition; Done returns to onboarding. PDF export also works through the existing writer. Or let the Pagewright arrange a finished Page. |
| Story name | The Book suggests a role. Its description explains the role through familiar activities. The reader can reject the first suggestion. |
| First Pages | Choose letters, little adventures, everyday wonders, weather and place, mystery, or funny oddities. Previews show the sort of Page to expect. |
| Tone | Choose a gentle invitation, a nudge, or a challenge. This affects the Book's tone, and invitations can still be refused. |
| Chapter | Read the five Chapters' plain descriptions and choose a starting Chapter. |
| Wicker | Meet Wicker, choose a reply, and roll for the result. His story consequence does not remove the reader's saved words. |
| First little book | Read what the Book remembers, choose a cover, optionally add a photograph, and make a PDF. Then choose reminder timing, including no notifications. |

## A few lines from the new copy

The first writing prompt:

> Look up from the screen. Notice one thing near you: a sound, the light, your mug, how your body feels. Write one sentence about what you actually notice.

Zara's snack question:

> “What do you like to eat while you read?” Zara asks. “Mine's green apples. The crunch annoys the librarian.”
>
> “Write a snack below. 'Nothing' counts too. This is how the Book starts learning little things about you.”

The belief question:

> “Tell me one thing you believe,” Zara says. “Something you care about enough to say it out loud.”
>
> “I believe people can change. Wicker says that's because I haven't met enough people.”

The surprise choice:

> The Book writes, “Oh, good.” A tiny wolf walks out of the margin, sniffs your sentence, and falls asleep on it. The Book had other plans. The wolf did not.

The Pagewright:

> The Pagewright holds up the scissors. “I can arrange it for you, too. I have excellent taste. Zara has concerns.”

The tone question:

> “How would you like the Book to speak to you?” Zara asks. “Gentle invitations, a little encouragement, or a proper challenge? This changes the tone of its Pages.”
>
> The Book writes: “I can be gentle. I've practised on a moth.”

## The first Pages after onboarding

- **Welcome:** names the Book as speaker, explains that Gemma is an AI running on the phone, names the download button, describes progress and its reading/writing check, and explains that the reader can continue without it.
- **Gemma ready:** explains what passed and how the Book can use kept words in later stories. The reader's own words remain the record of their real day.
- **First little job:** notice something, tap Capture, write a sentence, tap Keep. Body, Place, Radio, Glow, and Contents are described in ordinary terms.
- **This month's story:** says clearly that a story may already have started and offers a catch-up.
- **Picture Page:** explains choosing a photograph, adding fictional words and decoration, previewing it, and keeping it.
- **Calendar:** says access is optional, explains how plans help choose Pages, and names the permission request.
- **Wonder Compass:** explains a run as a sequence of real tasks, what to bring back, and how to continue.

The first PDF uses “Your First Pages” on its cover and a small Book illustration in place of the door emblem. Its recap and letter language follow the same clear wording as onboarding. Internal edition and archive identifiers remain compatible.

## Research and validation

The science copy uses ordinary examples, following the landing page's Curse section. It no longer extrapolates mind-wandering reports into years of lost life or treats the three memory answers as proof of a diagnosis. The sources remain visible beside the explanations:

- [Killingsworth & Gilbert, *Science* (2010)](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/KILLINGSWORTH%20%26%20GILBERT%20%282010%29.pdf)
- [Simons & Chabris, *Perception* (1999)](https://www.chabris.com/Simons1999.pdf)
- [Avni-Babad & Ritov (2003)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14640847/)

The optional habit panel describes the [Lally et al. habit study](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/ejsp.674) and the [Jose, Lim & Bryant diary study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17439760.2012.671345) without promising a thirty-day transformation or claiming these studies tested this app.

Swift syntax parsing and diff whitespace checks passed. Static checks confirmed saved keys, choice identifiers, flow counts, and the full Pagewright callbacks. No build or tests requiring a build were run. Phone/iPad layout, actual taps, permission dialogs, Pagewright exports, and the finished PDF still need a running-app walkthrough.
