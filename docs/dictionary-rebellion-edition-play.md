# Dictionary Rebellion: Play Leaves for the Bound Editions

Authored 2026-08-23. This is the content master for the September edition-play
packet. It contains finished reader-facing copy, puzzle answers, physical page
requirements, and delivery rules. The Swift models and PDF compositors should
implement this packet; they should not rewrite it at binding time.

## The promise

The living Book has already gathered and interpreted the period. These leaves
are where it stops talking and gives the paper away.

The Book authors the invitation. The Reader makes the final object. Nothing
written, colored, pasted, cut, solved, hidden, or contradicted on these leaves
is collected afterward.

There is no scan-back prompt, Keep button, completion mark, answer field,
achievement, or future Book callback. The bound edition is the destination.

## How September supplies the bindings

| Cadence | September contribution | Delivery |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | `dictionary-rebellion-coloring-page` | Guaranteed in the first eligible issue whose seven-day window overlaps the live event. Once per event occurrence. |
| Monthly | Five-leaf pool | Choose two, with different forms. Prefer material from phases that crossed the edition interval. |
| Seasonal | `atlas-of-one-escaped-word` | One candidate in the season-scale pool when the volume contains September and the event was available. |
| Annual | `the-word-the-year-changed` | One candidate in the year-scale pool when the event occurred during the year. |

The weekly premiere belongs to the weekly issue and does not automatically
repeat in the monthly, seasonal, or annual volume. The same is true of the
monthly leaves. Larger volumes receive their own scale of play.

The monthly pool is deliberately mixed:

- making: `order-17b-blackout`
- puzzle: `rule-is-escaping`
- scrapbook: `evidence-that-a-word-changed`
- cut-out: `the-placard-press`
- journal: `amendment-to-the-seventeenth-edition`

A monthly edition chooses no more than one leaf from the same form. If physical
space cannot hold two without violating the binding limit, it keeps the
stronger eligible leaf and prints no filler.

### Order 17-B Has Too Many Words

**ID:** `order-17b-blackout`  
**Form:** blackout poem / making  
**Event phase:** `outbreak` / Definitions Peel  
**Cadence:** monthly  
**Template slot:** `after-world-event`  
**Delivery:** pool  
**Footprint:** one page; ordinary content may follow on the reverse

**Contents listing**

> Order 17-B · a compulsory notice in need of a large black pencil

**Leaf copy**

> ORDER 17-B HAS TOO MANY WORDS
>
> Black out most of it. Leave behind what the paper was trying to confess.

The following notice is set as one generous block with enough leading for a
thick pencil or marker:

> All words currently absent from their appointed meanings are required to
> return before the closing bell. Each word must bring its original definition,
> any borrowed punctuation, a complete account of where it went, and the name
> of anyone who encouraged it. Unlicensed wonder will be confiscated. Ordinary
> things must remain ordinary. Later must arrive at an approved time. Fine must
> report that nothing is wrong. Home must stay in one place. Rest must show
> proof it was earned. Attention may not be spent without a receipt. Any word
> found carrying a private meaning will be corrected in red and filed beneath
> the nearest acceptable silence. Exceptions may be made for weather,
> promises, names spoken kindly, and small truths that refuse the assigned
> shelf. By order of the Seventeenth Edition. This paper believes it is
> finished.

At the foot of the page:

> THE PAPER CONFESSED:
>
> ________________________________________________________________

There is no canonical result and no answer key. The remaining words belong to
the Reader.

**ID:** `evidence-that-a-word-changed`  
**Form:** scrapbook / collage  
**Event phase:** `afterimage` / New Definitions Dry  
**Cadence:** monthly  
**Template slot:** `reader-worktable`  
**Delivery:** pool; prefer when the event was witnessed, but do not require it  
**Footprint:** one gutter-safe facing spread

**Contents listing**

> Evidence That a Word Changed · a case file with the glue still wet

**Spread opener**

> EVIDENCE THAT A WORD CHANGED
>
> Pick one word that did not mean quite the same thing by the end of September.
> If none did, choose one you do not quite trust.

The spread contains five loose, differently shaped evidence regions:

> THE WORD

> THE OLD MEANING

> WHAT HAPPENED TO IT

> A RECEIPT, LABEL, PHOTOGRAPH, SMALL PROOF, OR DRAWING

> WHAT IT MEANS NOW

Across the open center field:

> Tape. Tear. Draw arrows. Contradict the labels. The page has been told to
> hold still.

The spread may use empty tape corners, evidence tags, red correction marks,
and scraps from the Dictionary Rebellion's existing marginalia cabinet. It
must not prefill a personal fact or reproduce Reader-authored archive text.

## Editorial and production checks

Before this packet moves from content into implementation:

- The coloring-page transcription must be checked against the final asset.
- Every activity must remain usable without the app open.
- Monthly selection must choose two distinct forms, never two writing sheets
  wearing different headings.
- The word ladder must retain the exact six-word chain in its bound answer key.
- Blackout source prose must remain identical between the content definition
  and the rendered leaf.
- Cut-out and coloring leaves require blank reverses.
- Facing spreads must keep writing, cutting, and pasting targets clear of the
  gutter.
- No leaf may imply that the Book will see what the Reader does afterward.
- No leaf may claim a word changed, returned, or mattered unless the instruction
  leaves the Reader free to decide that it did not.
- Sparse bindings may omit optional pool leaves. Silence beats filler.
