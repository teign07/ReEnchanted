# ReEnchanted: audience and business-model recommendation

Research date: September 21, 2026. Decision memo, not an implementation change.

## Recommendation

Sell a continuing, participatory literary experience. Keep a useful free Book and offer a substantial introduction to the paid story. Retain paid monthly stories at the existing starting price, with printing available separately or through the Bound Year upgrade. Do not make physical fulfillment mandatory or promise that all future authored content will be free.

The first customer should be an adult who enjoys whimsical fantasy, wants a small creative pastime, and wants ordinary experiences to become something worth remembering. Prior purchases of narrative games, illustrated journals, creative prompt books, or stories by mail are better recruitment signals than simply following BookTok. Seek people who enjoy making choices and contributing details but find sustained writing or elaborate game setup burdensome.

This is a researched targeting hypothesis, not a measured ReEnchanted customer profile. There is no meaningful reader, conversion, retention, or sales data yet. The evidence supports a plausible initial market; it cannot establish product-market fit or the most profitable price.

## What the product actually offers

The current product direction connects real-life attention, small actions, a Book with its own character, an authored cast and world, remembered reader contributions, and personal editions. That combination is the commercial proposition to demonstrate.

The existing offer already leaves capture, the private archive, Pagewright, and personal PDFs free. The Digital Standing Order charges for newly authored monthly stories, rather than for the basic ability to turn memories into a Book. The Bound Year includes that subscription plus three seasonal softcovers and a final hardcover. Current listed prices are $9.99/month or $79.99/year for digital, and $24.99/month or $249/year for the Bound Year. These are local offer definitions, not verified transactions or demonstrated willingness to pay. See [landing-page plans](../LandingPage/index.html) and [BoundYearPricing/BoundYearCycle](../Shared/MonthlyEdition.swift).

Consequently, the paid story must create a reason to return beyond the free personal Book. A larger feature list will not establish that reason. A recognizable character, an intriguing unfinished situation, and a satisfying consequence of the reader's choice can.

### Direct competition changes the originality claim

[Lorbo](https://lorbo.app/) explicitly advertises real-life messages becoming fantasy chapters, a continuing cast, and an optional bound book. It lists a $9.99/month story plan but is currently free in alpha. Its positioning overlaps substantially with “your life becomes a fantasy novel”; its advertised prices are not evidence of successful monetization.

[Lore](https://chapterof.me/) likewise markets personal entries becoming an ongoing literary story and currently describes free access. Its public page does not establish paid demand or physical fulfillment.

ReEnchanted should therefore demonstrate its specific Book character, meaningful participation, adventures carried into actual life, and continuity grounded in what the reader contributed. A generic diary-to-fantasy transformation is already an advertised category.

### Lorbo's age and maturity

Lorbo's [press kit](https://lorbo.app/press) states that it was founded in January 2026 and was in private alpha as of May 2026. Its [About page](https://lorbo.app/about) and homepage still describe a small alpha. As of this research date, that is roughly eight to nine months since its stated founding, not eight to nine months of proven paid operation. The founding date is self-reported. Focused public searches did not establish an independent launch date, named founder biographies, subscriber counts, revenue, retention, or fulfilled print orders. Absence of accessible evidence is not proof that those things do not exist.

### Live website comparison

Both public sites were inspected in a desktop browser, including their visible first screens. Lorbo's prologue was opened successfully. ReEnchanted's cover, title page, first product page, and complete reading view were inspected. This was not a conversion experiment, mobile audit, or test of either private app.

**Judgment: Lorbo currently explains its offer better. ReEnchanted has a more distinctive visual presentation.** Lorbo connects a familiar action to a near-term result and a later physical reward immediately, then supplies a story sample and a clear invitation. ReEnchanted's default experience opens with an illustrated monthly cover, a title page, then a product explanation. Its 23-page first reading does contain concrete interactions and examples, but the visitor must navigate before understanding the central offer. The label for a monthly story can also initially resemble the title of a book being sold.

The difference is larger than prose length. ReEnchanted introduces proprietary names, several kinds of activities, several time scales, and several print formats. The visitor is being asked both to understand an unfamiliar product and to learn how its website works.

For scale, DOM text measurements found approximately 960 words in Lorbo's main landing content before opening the prologue, including FAQ answer text. ReEnchanted's optional complete-public-edition article contained approximately 8,200 words. These are different reading surfaces: the latter combines the shorter first journey with deeper product information, so an eightfold ratio should not be presented as a matched measure of mandatory reading. The first-screen comparison is the stronger evidence.

The best part of Lorbo's demonstration is that reading the sample is the product experience. Its weakness is that the sample only substitutes pronouns into an authored prologue; it does not demonstrate a visitor's actual day becoming a compelling story. ReEnchanted's prepared public Pages likewise do not prove personalized generation. Both companies still need to demonstrate the personalized payoff honestly.

Recommended front-door sequence for ReEnchanted:

1. Explain the experience beside the Book immediately: a living magic Book, small real-life adventures, a story and keepsake made together; iPhone/iPad and the starting offer in plain words.
2. Offer one brief, complete interaction. Show the reader's contribution, a character response, and a consequence or returned memory. Clearly distinguish a prepared example from live app behavior.
3. Show one readable resulting spread and the option to keep it on paper.
4. Present one invitation to start. Put the full world, detailed systems, and print catalogue behind optional exploration.

Keep the Book's art, page handling, and voice. Reduce the amount the visitor must understand before the first payoff. The website need not become a conventional software template to communicate clearly.

## The first customer and the experience to sell

Start with English-speaking adult iPhone users buying for themselves. For early physical orders, a US cohort would simplify fulfillment; this is an operational choice, not evidence that demand is confined to the US.

Recruit by observable behavior:

- They have paid for an imaginative hobby or narrative experience, rather than merely liking attractive fantasy images.
- They want some participation: choices, a small discovery, a photograph, a sentence, or an occasional excursion.
- They like keeping meaningful things but struggle with a blank journal or a complicated creative routine.
- They are comfortable with clearly disclosed AI assistance and want their own words and memories preserved distinctly.

My proposed customer promise is: **A mischievous magic Book that draws you into small adventures and keeps the story you make together.** Show that in one concrete sequence: the reader notices something real, contributes it, receives a distinctive response, makes a choice, and later sees that contribution matter again. Show a physical spread containing recognizable reader material as the eventual keepsake.

The likely purchase is a combination of anticipation, personal significance, manageable participation, and attachment to the Book. This interpretation comes from the evidence above; it still needs testing with actual readers.

Do not assume everyone in the adjacent communities is a match. Some paper journalers value doing the handwriting and decoration themselves. Some solo players want to author every scene. Some collectors specifically value human-made art: Apothecaria explicitly labels its work as using no generative AI. ReEnchanted's existing AI disclosure belongs in honest demonstrations, not after purchase. Source quality and authorship are commercial considerations for this audience.

Prioritize the person who wants the experience over a gift purchaser choosing it for someone else. A personalized Book requires the recipient's participation; a beautiful gift cannot supply that motivation.

## Packaging and economics

Use one primary invitation to begin the Book. Introduce the continuing paid story after a substantial sample, then present paper as an optional format once the reader can inspect their own edition. Keep the existing prices as starting hypotheses; this research does not justify a price increase or establish an optimum. A 30-day story sample is a proposed offer, not a claim that it is currently implemented.

Retain the free archive and creation tools. Keep the Bound Year available, but move the detailed weekly/monthly/seasonal/annual print catalogue away from the first purchase decision. The main simplification is sequencing decisions and making the paid story understandable.

Illustrative annual contribution, before acquisition, fixed content production, founder time, taxes, and overhead:

| Offer | Explicit assumptions | Contribution |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Digital at $79.99 | 15% Apple commission, if eligible/enrolled; $5 variable service cost | $62.99 |
| Bound Year at $249 | 2.9% + $0.30 domestic-card processing, 0.7% Billing fee, and $120 for all four books' manufacturing, shipping, replacements, and variable support | $119.74 |

The $5 and $120 costs are scenarios, not measured costs. Small Business enrollment has not been verified; ordinary first-year Apple commission would reduce the digital result. Stripe rates assume standard US card pricing, and actual contracted fees or additional products may differ. Sources: [Apple subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/), [Stripe payments](https://stripe.com/pricing), [Stripe Billing](https://stripe.com/billing/pricing).

On those assumptions, mandatory paper needs about 53% as many purchasers as digital-only to match its contribution, before crediting optional print purchases in the digital model. Physical-only might win; it is not mathematically disqualified. But no evidence yet shows it will preserve that conversion rate, and it adds fulfillment obligations before we know readers will make enough personally valued material.

Similarly, making all monthly stories free needs **additional** print contribution to replace lost digital contribution. Thirty annual digital subscribers contribute approximately $1,890 in this scenario. Replacing that requires about 16 additional Bound Year subscribers above those who would already have bought, or about 54 additional book orders at an assumed $35 contribution each. Neither referral volume nor print conversion has been measured.

Bound Year contribution already includes digital access; never add a second digital contribution for the same subscriber.

## Shared-world growth and the decision to revisit

A common monthly world is promising because people can discuss characters, surprises, choices, and discoveries without exposing their private lives. Free access alone does not create such a conversation. Let the free introduction encounter the current world; offer a public sample or invitation tied to the monthly event; make a finished nonprivate souvenir understandable outside the app. These are recommendations, not implemented acquisition results.

Recruit the first small cohort through solo-story, cozy narrative, and creative-journaling communities and their reviewers. Present an honest demonstration of the full loop and the AI boundary. Measure return after the initial novelty, willingness to pay for the next authored story, the proportion who want their actual edition printed, and contribution after fulfillment. No outreach, advertising, or enrollment was performed for this memo.

The recommendation should change if paid trials show that readers consistently value paper more than the continuing story, and that the extra physical conversion and retention cover fulfillment. Conversely, if readers enjoy the free Book but do not want another authored episode or their own printed volume, pricing simplification will not repair the missing demand.

This research establishes a credible adjacent market and a sharper first-customer hypothesis. It does not establish that the current app delivers the experience clearly or that a physical proof meets collectible-book expectations. Source review and desk research were performed; no build, runtime journey test, customer interview, paid acquisition test, or physical proof was performed.
