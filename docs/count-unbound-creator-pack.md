## Ordinary school

These scenes establish an Academy worth inhabiting. They can also serve as an evergreen arrival sequence outside October with the Sunday-date reference omitted. Reading all of them is never a prerequisite for the monthly story. October's opening offers the first three across October 1–3; the ribbon rehearsal precedes the Jump on October 4. A reader may leave a scene waiting.

### The chair that gets there first

**Direction:** `academy.school.chair-first`. Kitchens. Meet Ambrose, Serenity, and Wicker through lunch. One optional fictional choice; each result rejoins the final paragraph. No romance verdict or event participation.

**Read**

I've found the Kitchens.

There was a smell coming through my back cover. I followed it. You can follow me.

Ambrose Trencher stands behind a pot big enough to bathe a professor. He's fishing something out with a ladle.

“Bay leaf,” he says.

The thing has legs.

“Ambrose.”

“Then it can leave under its own steam.”

He sets it on the windowsill. It goes.

Serenity Brown has taken a place halfway down the table. Her map is spread between the salt and a heel of bread. There are mountains on it, a river, and a small pencilled sea where she's spilled her water.

She adds a boat.

“Weather,” she says, and moves her bowl uphill.

Wicker Eddies pulls out the chair opposite her.

“That seat's taken.”

“By whom?”

“Someone I haven't met yet.”

“Excellent. They'll be late.”

He sits. Serenity draws a bog on his side of the map.

Wicker steals the pencil. He draws stepping stones across it, each one just too far from the next.

Serenity studies them. Then she draws a very small Wicker in the water.

He leans closer.

“My hair's better than that.”

“It's wet.”

Ambrose puts a bowl between them. Both reach for the bread. Neither lets go.

**Where do you sit?**

- **Beside the map.** Serenity shifts the sea away from your sleeve. “Mind the ferry. It charges whatever it likes.”
- **Near Ambrose.** He puts an extra spoon by your bowl. “For the thing you didn't order and will want when you see it.”
- **At the end, where there's room.** The bench scoots under you. It has been trying to join the table all morning.

Wicker tears the bread. He gives Serenity the larger piece without looking at it.

She notices. She puts her smaller piece on his map-bog.

“Land,” she says. “Try to stay on it.”

I keep the crumbs that fall between my pages. Ambrose says he'll shake me out later. He says that every time.

### A sentence with its coat off

**Direction:** `academy.school.sentence-coat`. Ink-Binding with Vivian Villanelle. Demonstrate one memorable sentence by revising fictional material, never by silently rewriting the reader's contribution. This opens the first weekly crossing.

**Read**

Professor Villanelle has written a sentence on the board.

*The ineffable splendour of dawn bestowed a profound sense of possibility upon the weary traveller.*

Something behind the plaster coughs.

Villanelle waits until we've all read it.

“What happened?”

Penny Blackletter raises her hand.

“Someone got up.”

“Where?”

“The sentence hasn't said.”

“What did they see?”

Penny looks at the board again. “It hasn't said that either.”

Villanelle rubs out *ineffable*. The chalk squeals. She keeps rubbing.

“I was at the bus stop,” she says. “I had a headache. Someone had left an orange on the bench.”

She wipes the rest away.

*There was an orange on the wet bench, bright enough that I nearly missed the bus.*

The thing in the wall stops coughing.

I like the orange. I can get my teeth round it.

Serenity has been working on a sentence of her own. Wicker bends sideways to read it.

She puts her hand over the page.

“Ask.”

“Is it about me?”

“It was about something that needed throwing out. I can see why you're hopeful.”

Villanelle reaches between them and takes Wicker's unused paper.

“Your turn.”

He looks at the blank. For once, it gets the first word.

### The wrong way to the classroom

**Direction:** `academy.school.wrong-way`. Introduce Soren's chemistry with Serenity and his tendency to arrange rather than ask. Choice establishes a shared occasion, not a romantic commitment. All branches are recorded as fiction choices.

**Read**

Serenity has lost the classroom.

She knows where it was. She drew a good map yesterday. The corridor has taken this personally.

“We could ask,” I say.

“We're almost there.”

We've passed the same boot three times. It's growing confident.

On the next windowsill sits a paper bird. One wing points left. Written under it are the words *if you want the room*. On the other wing: *if you want the long way*.

Serenity turns it over. There's a tiny drawing of her boat underneath.

“Soren.”

She sounds pleased. Then she frowns at the two wings.

“He could've waited for us.”

Soren Ng is sitting on the stair beyond the left-hand door. He has two chalk stubs and an expression that was clearly meant to look accidental.

“The long way has a glass roof,” he says.

“You put my boat on a bird.”

“It needed to get upstairs.”

She sits beside him to examine the fold. He shows her where the paper turns. Their hands meet at the wing; he lets her finish it.

Wicker comes through the classroom door behind them.

“How elaborate. Have you tried saying hello?”

“Hello, Wicker,” Soren says.

Serenity laughs into the bird.

**Which way do you suggest?**

- **“Show us the glass roof.”** Soren stands too quickly, drops a chalk stub, and has to retrieve it from under Serenity's shoe. She lifts her foot slowly.
- **“Serenity can choose.”** She looks at the classroom, then at the bird. “Roof. We already know what a room looks like.”
- **“Class first. Roof after.”** “Someone write that down,” Wicker says. “A plan with an after.” He holds the classroom door while they collect the chalk.

I nip the tail of the paper bird when nobody's looking. It nips my ribbon back.

### The ribbon lesson

**Direction:** `academy.school.ribbon-lesson`. October 4 before the Jump; evergreen outside this run. Introduce Permancer's competence and the real Jump anchor. Choosing to enter continues the authored Book Jump; remaining outside gives the report path. No Belief purchase is required for this supervised lesson under the proposed policy.

**Read**

Professor Permancer lays a ribbon across the floor.

“That end stays here.”

The ribbon immediately crawls towards his shoe.

“Approximately here.”

He kneels, makes a loop, and passes the end through it. He does it again slowly. Penny copies him. Wicker produces a knot that has trapped his thumb.

Permancer frees it without comment.

“Pick a detail from outside the Academy,” he says. “Something you can name exactly. You'll carry it with you. Your Book will keep its place.”

I open to a clean leaf. If you've already kept a sentence for this, I can hold that one.

Permancer touches the ribbon. A little door opens above the knot. Through it I can see his office, including a cup he has been searching for since breakfast.

He puts his hand through, retrieves it, and comes back.

“Now,” he says. “Where was my way home?”

Everyone points at the ribbon.

“Good. Keep looking at it when there's something more interesting to look at.”

**Choose:** Enter the supervised Jump / Stay in the Vault.

**Direction:** “Stay” must work. It earns no Jump attendance record. The Academy's incident occurs independently; a report preserves the clue about the headcount. The app must never ask for a real-life sentence merely to allow departure from fiction.

## The six shared scenes

### 1. The Sunday Jump

**Direction:** `count-unbound.scene.sunday-jump`. October 4, SETUP / `school-hours`. Split into authored Jump beats at the choices and emergency return. Dialogue and setting below assume explicit fictional entry. Outside readers receive the report in the integration notes. The chosen ordinary anchor is available only within its permission scope.

**Read**

The Vault has polished its handles for Sunday. Even the dangerous ones are keeping still.

Penny checks the list beside the open copy of *Dracula*.

“Permancer. Wicker. You. Me.”

“Still no Dracula?” Wicker asks.

“He lives there.”

“Then he can make the tea.”

“We don't accept food,” Permancer says. “Or shelter. We follow the entrance hall to the first landing. Then we return.”

Ribbons run from his bookmark to each name on Penny's list. I have yours. Don't pull; I'm holding something at the other end.

**What do you check?**

- **The knot.** It tightens under your finger.
- **Your name on the list.** Its ink is warm. You check the other names too.
- **The way home.** From this side, the doorway shows the Vault. From the other, you can see a long table in the dark.

Permancer steps through. Penny follows. The ribbon draws you into the crease.

Words race beneath your shoes. Then stone catches your feet.

The table is longer than it looked. An orange sits at the far end under an unlit chandelier.

I put a corner out to see it better. Penny catches me.

“Observe,” she whispers.

I was observing with my mouth.

A door opens. Dracula comes in.

“You have come a long way.”

He stops beside the table. One hand rests on the chair. A paragraph holds him there.

Wicker moves sideways. Dracula's eyes don't follow.

“He can't see us properly.”

“He's held at this passage,” Permancer says. “It lets the lesson enter without changing his next action.”

“You've pinned him.”

“Wicker. Put the pen away.”

But the pen is already down in the gutter. Wicker writes:

> For one sentence, let him choose the verb.

The table draws in its legs. The orange rolls.

Dracula looks at Wicker. Then at you. Then at the light behind you.

“What follows?” he asks.

**What do you do?**

- **Tell Wicker to cross it out.** His nib tears the paper. The words hold.
- **Call Permancer.** The professor is already pulling the ribbon.
- **Watch Dracula.** His hand stays on the chair. His shadow lets go.

The chandelier burns black.

“Penny, through. You next.”

I pull the ribbon towards the Vault. A word catches in my stitching. I leave it there. I need both covers for this.

Penny reaches the light. You follow.

Wicker backs towards the doorway.

“I gave you one verb.”

Dracula smiles.

“Follow.”

Wicker crosses. Permancer holds the way open behind him.

“Back through. All of you.”

The words reach farther than the ribbon.

Then the professor is home. The source slams shut.

Penny runs her finger down the list. Every ribbon answers. Every named traveller is back.

“Complete.”

Wicker sits on the floor. There's ink on his knee.

At supper he asks where Serenity is.

“Finishing her map,” Ambrose says. “The East Stacks.”

Wicker takes the bowl Ambrose gives him. It goes cold in his hands.

Under the East Stacks door, a shadow slips past the light. Its owner isn't on Penny's list.

**Direction:** Wicker's supper question shows an already-existing interest, not knowledge of an attack. The registered return is true. Emergency return commits once. The outside-reader report excludes the supper question unless delivered as a separate witnessed scene.

### 2. The East Stacks Incident

**Direction:** `count-unbound.scene.east-stacks`. October 8, BUILDUP / `uninvited`. No sexualized bite or Dracula romance. Wicker's concern complicates an established attachment; Serenity's experience stays central. Soren's later visit is separate from the immediate rescue.

**Read**

A book lands in Ambrose's soup.

He fishes it out by the spine. *Doors That Should Stay Metaphors.* It drips on his shoes.

Another book falls in the hall. Then another, farther away.

Ambrose puts down the ladle.

The East Stacks have squeezed their aisles shut. Penny goes sideways between them, following a trail of catalogue cards.

Each says the same thing.

> HE WAS HERE.

“Serenity?” she calls.

Ambrose reaches the gap first. He kneels between Travel and Unreliable Geography.

Serenity is against the bottom shelf. Her map lies open beside her. Two small wounds show at her neck.

“She's breathing. Bring a clean cloth.”

Wicker turns so sharply he hits the shelf. He comes back with three cloths and an empty water jug. He looks at the jug. Goes again.

Permancer's bookmark pulls towards Serenity, then the wall.

“A living anchor.”

Penny catches his sleeve. “Explain.”

“A person from a story has used her to hold himself here.”

“I gave him—” Wicker begins.

“Water first,” Ambrose says.

Serenity opens her eyes.

“Will someone stop turning that page?”

The books are all closed.

She reaches across the floor.

**What do you do?**

- **Move her map nearer.** Her hand closes on its edge.
- **Offer Penny's pencil.** Serenity draws a short line, stops, and points it at the north wall.
- **Make room.** You move the fallen books. Ambrose can get beside her without stepping on the map.

“His fingers were coming apart,” she says. “I told him he shouldn't be here.”

She looks at the cloth in Ambrose's hand.

“He said he'd found a way to stay.”

Wicker sets the water down. A little spills. He wipes it with his sleeve before anyone can ask.

Serenity sees him.

“What did you write?”

He tells her. The exact sentence. I keep it too.

She shuts her eyes again.

Wicker reaches towards her hand, then puts his own flat on the floor.

“Get my map,” she says to Ambrose.

The Kitchens door appears at the end of the aisle. It has come a long way and left its hinges behind.

Ambrose lifts her. She keeps the map.

A black line travels across its sea, through a mountain, and into the north margin.

Serenity turns her head.

“He's there.”

Permancer takes a step towards the wall.

“Tomorrow,” she says.

He stops.

“Yes. Tomorrow.”

Wicker holds the Kitchens door. Serenity goes through without looking at him.

He stays with the handle long after Ambrose has put her down.

### Between lessons: the map with room left

**Direction:** `count-unbound.school.map-room`. October 10–14 after the incident. Ordinary-school continuation; introduces Soren's care through their existing map-making pleasure. Never required for the main plot. Reader may influence one later social occasion through the separate invitation scene below.

**Read**

Soren brings a sheet of tracing paper to the Kitchens.

Serenity is making a tower out of toast crusts. It leans. She's named it after Permancer.

“I've copied the coast,” Soren says.

Her face closes a little.

“For the investigation?”

“For the boat.”

He puts the paper down. He's drawn her little boat in six impossible harbours. One is in a tree.

Serenity looks at them for a long time.

“It'll need a ladder.”

“I wasn't sure how tall.”

She takes his pencil.

I start to close. She puts one finger between my leaves.

“Stay. We need a cliff.”

So I stand on my fore-edge while she draws a ladder up me. It tickles.

At the doorway, Wicker arrives with a repaired map case under his arm.

He sees Soren's paper. He turns the case so the repair faces his coat.

Ambrose takes it from him.

“Tea?”

“No.”

“Then stop standing in the draught.”

Wicker takes a seat at the far end. For the whole of one cup of tea, he has nothing to say about Soren's harbours.

### 3. The Man Reading Himself

**Direction:** `count-unbound.scene.man-reading-himself`. October 15–17, BUILDUP. One chosen question, with the shared continuation supplying essential canon that the other branches do not deliver. No branch can leave the braid assuming an unasked question.

**Read**

“That one,” Serenity says.

The old reading-room door stands open. Inside, Dracula sits under a green lamp, reading *Dracula*.

He has a pencil. I dislike this before he's done anything with it.

Beside a paragraph he's written:

> He wouldn't have said this.

Penny leans round the door.

“You wrote that?”

“I was present.”

“You can still be wrong.”

Serenity steps into the light. Dracula rises.

“Miss Brown.”

“Serenity.”

He says it correctly. She waits.

Dracula puts his pencil down.

“I've read the last page.”

His hand rests on the closed half of the book.

“Every morning returns me to a sentence I have already lived. You leave a room and something can happen to you. I leave mine because the next paragraph says I must.”

“So you hurt me,” Serenity says.

“I was disappearing.”

“You knew I was a person.”

He looks at her bandage.

“Yes.”

**What do you ask?**

- **“What would you do with a tomorrow?”** He turns the pencil between his fingers. “Something I haven't read.” Serenity says, “That's an empty answer.” He puts the pencil down again.
- **“Why her?”** “She saw me. A witness held me for a moment. I made the moment last.” Serenity pulls out the chair opposite him and sits. He has to look at her now.
- **“Would you do it again?”** The lamp buzzes. “If I were vanishing?” he says. “Yes.” Penny writes the word. She doesn't offer him a softer one.

Dracula opens his book.

“I want a future tense. Yours has been waiting for you all day.”

“And if it costs someone else?” Penny asks.

“I intend to remain.”

Serenity stands. “Then we'd better learn how to send you back.”

His shadow moves first.

It touches the shelf. One book opens onto the sea. Another lets snow into the room. Roses push through a third and catch on Penny's sleeve.

Dracula steps backwards between them.

For a moment he stands in three different doorways.

Then the books close.

Serenity unfolds her map. The black line has split into branches.

Penny pulls a rose thorn from her cuff.

“How many ways?”

“More than yesterday.”

I keep the fallen thorn. It has actual business here now.

### 4. The Wrong Castle

**Direction:** `count-unbound.scene.wrong-castle`. October 22, CLIMAX / `reciprocal`. Weekly other-side finding may make the authored observation branch more specific, but the rule is always independently discoverable. The three selected rooms have small immediate effects and rejoin canon.

**Read**

The East Stacks grow a tower overnight.

The bottom is library. The top has stolen a nursery window. Between them, a stair keeps asking for another floor.

A drawbridge hangs over the Quillquarium. The nibs underneath are furious. They've never agreed so completely.

Penny pins up a notice:

> THE EAST STACKS ARE SEVERAL PLACES. PLEASE USE ONE AT A TIME.

Permancer ties ribbons to the banister. Wicker holds the source volume. Serenity studies her map.

“I'm coming,” she says.

“I was about to ask,” Permancer says.

“You're very slow.”

She folds the map into her repaired case. Wicker watches the clasp close. She catches him looking and goes ahead.

Inside, the stair turns into a corridor. There are three ways through.

**Which way do you take?**

- **The ballroom.** Your first step starts a waltz. The next starts something with drums. You cross to the beat you can follow; empty coats sweep past you.
- **The nursery.** A wooden star catches your sleeve. You unhook it carefully. It has grown one stone tooth.
- **The red chair.** Four doors bring you back to it. Serenity turns it to face the wall. The next door finally minds its own business.

Dracula waits in the central hall. A fireplace blows snow into his coat. Above him, the borrowed nursery window is trying to find morning.

“This isn't my castle.”

“No,” Serenity says. “These are everybody else's rooms.”

A door opens onto a boat in a storm. Permancer wrestles it shut. Seawater soaks his shoes.

“You damaged my source,” Dracula says.

Permancer keeps both hands on the handle.

“We did.”

Wicker opens the book to his sentence. Under it, a second hand has written FOLLOW.

“I thought—”

Serenity waits.

He looks at the map case. At the bandage above it.

“I wanted to see what would happen.”

“You have.”

He lowers his eyes to the page.

The wall splits. Under the stone is a thin fold of paper.

Serenity touches it. Her map pulls towards the gap.

“His way back.”

Permancer brings the bookmark near. Its ribbons reach towards the source, the gap, and Serenity.

Penny puts a card beside each.

SOURCE. INVITATION. LIVING CLAIM.

Dracula watches her write.

Serenity puts her own hand over the third card.

“That one's mine.”

Behind us, the sea knocks again.

### 5. A Future Tense

**Direction:** `count-unbound.scene.future-tense`. October 28, CLIMAX. Render opening → one invitation socket → one route insert → shared withdrawal → closing. The invitation sentence appears at most once in this scene. The route inserts never quote it again. Reader evidence never changes the shared outcome or supplies unreported visual facts.

**Read — opening**

The stair has reached the supper tables.

Ambrose moves the salt out of its way. A paper moon hangs over the Great Hall, torn from a book that keeps calling for it.

On the wooden stand, *Dracula* tries to close.

I wedge myself beneath the cover. It presses down. I have held worse things open. I don't tell it that.

Permancer reads the return list. This time he names everyone allowed through. Penny checks every ribbon from both sides.

Serenity stands by the final fold. The map case is open at her feet.

“He's coming.”

The paper moon goes dark.

Dracula descends the stair. He sees the open source, Serenity, and the invitation we've made.

**Direction — choose exactly one invitation socket:**

- Exact quotation allowed: `I open to the sentence you brought me. “{reader_invitation_sentence}” We have given the threshold that way of saying come in.`
- Kept finding, quotation withheld: `I keep your sentence shut. The threshold uses the invitation you brought back; your words stay with me.`
- No invitation finding: `Ambrose pulls a chair back from the table. The threshold takes its shape from the empty place.`

If the reader confirmed a specific invitation interpretation in preparation, follow with its authored enactment: **room made** → the threshold widens to leave a place; **light left** → a small lamp stays lit beyond the fold; **way held** → a paper door remains ajar; **something else** → use the generic threshold line above, never guess. These are fictional constructions, not claims about the original real place.

**Read — continue**

Dracula stops on the last stair.

He has noticed.

**Direction:** Insert Terms, Rule, or Ruse below. If the reader hasn't selected one, offer the three strategies now. Reading without choosing uses the public fallback. No branch chooses a strategy based solely on inferred personality.

**Read — shared withdrawal**

At the edge of the source, Dracula turns to Serenity.

“You would send me back.”

She unfolds her map. The black line runs from him into the little kingdom she drew before any of this happened.

“What you changed in me is mine now.”

She puts a finger beneath the line.

“It doesn't belong to you.”

The ink lifts like a loose thread.

“It doesn't hold you here.”

She pulls it free.

His second shadow goes out. The open book drags at his coat.

I hold. A stitch parts in my spine.

Dracula looks into the source. For once, he doesn't know what to say before the page turns.

It turns anyway.

**Direction:** For Ruse, Wicker closes the final fold here. All routes continue below.

**Read — closing**

The stair breaks into words. The ballroom sheds its extra centuries. The drawbridge lifts out of the Quillquarium with a flock of nibs hanging from it.

The borrowed moon sails back between two covers.

Then the Great Hall is a hall again.

I wriggle out from under *Dracula*. My loose stitch catches. Penny frees it with her pencil.

Serenity listens.

Somewhere far away, a closed book turns a page.

She picks up her map case.

“Still there,” she says.

Ambrose pulls out a chair.

This time, it's for her.

### 6. The First Nightbound Morning

**Direction:** `count-unbound.scene.first-nightbound-morning`. October 29, AFTERMATH / `nightbound`. Serenity names herself; Threshold Protocols begin; no romantic resolution is earned by October alone. Reader-specific social continuation follows separately.

**Read**

Morning finds Serenity in the Kitchens.

She's built the toast tower again. It has buttresses now.

Sunlight crosses her hands. She turns over a card. A moon. Another card. Another moon.

“Lazy,” she tells the deck.

Ambrose brings tea.

Permancer arrives with a phrase he's plainly been carrying all the way downstairs.

“Residual threshold sensitivity.”

Serenity looks up.

“Is that what the tea's called?”

He sits down.

“What would you call it?”

She looks at the pantry door. Behind it, too far away for anybody else, a page turns.

“Nightbound.”

Penny takes out a card. She waits until Serenity nods.

I make room for the word too.

Permancer puts a new notice on the table.

> Name every traveller. Close the list. Check both sides of the door.

Below that:

> No living anchors.

Serenity takes the pencil.

> Ask me before you make a map of what I can hear.

Permancer reads it and leaves it where she wrote it.

Wicker appears in the doorway. His hair is wet; he's apparently tried to improve it with water and lost.

“May I?”

Serenity turns a card face down.

“Not today.”

He takes his hand off the frame.

“All right.”

She watches until he's gone. Then she moves a piece of toast that was holding up the tower.

The tower falls. She laughs, once, at the mess.

“Terrible engineering.”

Ambrose brings more toast.

## Strategy interludes

One is normally read October 18–21 after explicit selection. The other efforts happen in public history without being credited as scenes the reader attended. Strategies affect the method of return; romantic preferences never choose them.

### Terms — Supper with a Finished Man

**Direction:** `count-unbound.interlude.terms-supper`.

**Read**

Ambrose invites Dracula to supper.

“In here?” Penny asks.

“The soup's in here.”

He pulls back a chair. Dracula stops beside it.

“Your invitation is imprecise.”

“Sit down and we'll improve it.”

Serenity takes the place opposite him. Ambrose fills her bowl, yours, his own. He leaves Dracula's empty.

“You presume I don't eat.”

“What would you like?”

Dracula looks at the soup.

“Nothing.”

Ambrose nods and passes the bread.

“I won't return to a finished life,” Dracula says.

Serenity sets down her spoon.

“You're staying by holding on to mine.”

He looks at her. She waits until he looks away.

Ambrose gets a sheet of paper from the recipe drawer. There's a butter mark in one corner.

**Which term do you name first?**

- **The Academy must admit what it did.** Ambrose writes that the door was opened and its return wrongly declared safe. He leaves space for Permancer's signature.
- **Dracula must name his act.** “I harmed Serenity deliberately,” the Count says at last. Penny writes every word. Serenity checks the line herself.
- **Future Jumps must change.** Ambrose writes a closed return list, a stated purpose, and a safeguard for both directions. “Professor can work out the knot,” he says.

They write the other terms beneath the first. Dracula reads them all.

“And in exchange, I go back.”

“And let go,” Serenity says.

He puts a finger on the butter mark.

“This was a recipe.”

“Still is,” Ambrose says. “We're trying to get everyone out of the room alive.”

The Count signs. Permancer will add his name at the return.

Ambrose passes dessert around the table. Dracula's bowl stays empty.

### Hunt — The Door's Autopsy

**Direction:** `count-unbound.interlude.hunt-autopsy`.

**Read**

Penny draws chalk around the place where the door stood.

“No body,” she says. “Unhelpful.”

Permancer lays out the list, the ribbon, the source, Wicker's sentence, and Serenity's map. Its case has a note attached: *back before supper*.

**Which do you put inside the outline first?**

- **Wicker's sentence.** Its ink pulls towards the source. “He could choose where to look,” Permancer says. Penny writes SOURCE.
- **The return invitation.** *All of you* appears inside the chalk. Permancer winces. Penny writes INVITATION.
- **Serenity's map.** A black branch reaches towards the outline and holds there. Penny writes LIVING CLAIM. “She's the only one who can let this go.”

The other cards follow. The floorboards shift them into a triangle.

The chalk rises, making a doorway in the air.

**Direction:** If a Week Three finding has returned, show its permitted exact sentence or private reference here, followed by: `Penny walks behind the chalk. “Let's see what it does from here.”` Otherwise she walks behind it on her own initiative. The discovery below happens in every case.

**Read — continue**

From Penny's side, the ribbons point inwards.

Permancer walks around to join her. For a long time, he doesn't speak.

“I kept standing where I'd always stood.”

Penny makes room for him.

“Now you're here.”

He reads the cards backwards, then forwards.

“Open his source. Give him an invitation he recognizes. Serenity withdraws the claim. The source can take him back.”

The chalk door turns towards the Great Hall. Beyond it, a chair waits pulled back from a table.

Penny puts an empty card under INVITATION.

“We've got somewhere to start.”

## Four weekly crossings

Offer one new crossing each week. These are four different invitations into life, not four compulsory steps. Older uncompleted asks don't accumulate on the desk. An earlier kept sentence may be reused only after the reader selects it. Each return receives its own immediate Book response and a later authored use, even if the reader does none of the others.

### Week One — Leave me something to hold

**Direction:** `count-unbound.mission.hold-place`. October 1–7; offered after the sentence lesson, also reachable from Jump preparation. No need to complete before October 4. Keep one true sentence or select a permitted existing reader contribution. A Jump uses it as an anchor only after explicit selection. Later returns still earn a quiet Book response and braid material; they cannot retroactively anchor a completed Jump.

**Read**

Before we go anywhere, show me one thing that's here.

The chipped rim of your cup. The sound the fridge makes before it stops. A mark on your hand you've never quite explained.

Pick something near you. Give me a sentence I could find it by.

I'll hold that end.

**Field label:** The thing, exactly as you noticed it.

**Kept response:** I've got it. We can go strange places with something this particular tied to us.

**Later use:** The chosen sentence can be the Jump anchor and the return recognition. Withheld quotations stay closed; use “the detail you gave me.” If nothing was supplied, the class uses its fictional return ribbon. A prepared Book anchor never becomes Serenity's living-anchor condition.

### Week Two — An Invitation Without Words

**Direction:** Preserve `count-unbound.mission.invitation-without-words`. First offered October 8–14 after the incident/report; remains reachable until October 27. Free text establishes a lived finding. Photograph supported. This is the only weekly finding that directly supplies the climax's invitation; other weeks have their own uses.

**Read**

Nobody's sitting in it. It still changes the room.

Find something that says come in without words. A light. A path. A place someone has made at a table.

Keep one sentence about how it invites you.

You can leave the place where it is. I want to carry the sentence.

**Field label:** It said come in by…

**Permission control:** Use my sentence in Academy scenes / Keep my wording inside my own Book.

**Kept response:** I can work with that. The Academy keeps drawing doorways. You've brought me an invitation.

**Preparation reply:** What should the paper threshold learn from it? A place made for someone / A light left for someone / A way held open / Something else.

**Direction:** The reply interprets the fictional construction; it does not rewrite the observation. If unanswered, use the generic invitation socket. Permission to use a finding is distinct from permission to quote it.

### Week Three — The other side

**Direction:** `count-unbound.mission.other-side`. First offered October 15–21; can return until October 27. Pair with the autopsy or a short Penny encounter reachable on every strategy. Inspect a familiar object from another permitted, ordinary viewpoint. No trespass, forced entry, or need to investigate an actual person.

**Read**

Pick something you know from one side. Turn over the mug. Look behind the chair. Read the back of the thing you've been carrying.

**Field label:** From this side, I found…

**Kept response:** Oh. Keep that beside the first look. I want both.

**Later use, Penny encounter:** With permission, the Book shows the exact line. Otherwise it names “the other side you checked.” Penny turns a catalogue card over and writes BACK on it. “I'm adding a column.” She then checks both sides of the prepared threshold. This visit supplies the same small consequence on Terms and Gambit as on Hunt. It doesn't duplicate if already delivered inside the autopsy.

### Week Four — Go back once

**Direction:** `count-unbound.mission.go-back-once`. October 22–31. Select an earlier permitted personal finding; no earlier October return is required. The reader can choose any existing ordinary-life sentence, or notice a present detail and return to it later. This crossing concerns change or persistence in actual life, not evidence that the fictional incident altered the world.

**Read**

Go back to the thing it came from. The same window, the same cup, the same patch of path. Have another look.

Give me one sentence from this visit. It can have changed. It can be stubbornly the same.
