[p] The braid is real
[h2] Your kept pages become a Book of You.
[p] After enough real pages gather, the local Book brain braids them into a
            readable entry: the small true things you kept, the bargains you made,
            the weather outside, and the fictional choices that answered back.
[p] It is not a recap feed. It is the day rewritten as something you can
            return to, share, and eventually bind into an edition.
[figcaption] A real Book of You entry, braided from kept pages and on-device context.

[p] Bound for keeps
[h2] Publish your own editions.
[p] Your kept pages don't just scroll away. When enough of them gather, the
            Book can bind them into real, shareable PDF volumes you publish for
            yourself — composed and rendered entirely on device.
[h3] The Monthly Edition
[p] At each month's turn, the Book gathers that month's kept pages — braids,
              one-sentence souvenirs, letters, the things it noticed — and binds them
              into a chaptered PDF with its own theme, cover, and a foreword the Book
              writes about the month you just lived.
[h3] The Annual Volume
[p] A whole year becomes one bound book: twelve monthly chapters, each in its
              own palette, wrapped in a year-long foreword, your constellations, and a
              closing. A year of ordinary life, made into a fairy tale you can hold.

[h2] Over
[h2] kinds of page.
[p] Every day, the Book offers you a deck of different page types to play
            with and keep. They ship in the base game, free forever — from quiet
            logs to playful little games. Here are a few of them.
[h3] Book Jumping
[p] At the Academy, students learn to physically jump into books and
              live them as if they were real. Step into a public-domain classic —
[em] The Wizard of Oz
[em] Dracula
[p] — and explore it from
              the inside, one controlled beat at a time. Bring back a souvenir
              sentence. Just don't go too deep.
[h3] Quips
[p] A tiny odd fact that tilts the day toward wonder, no work required.
[em] "An octopus is what happens when curiosity gets eight hands."
[em] "Rust is iron remembering it used to be in the ground."
[p] Keep the ones that land.
[h3] Playful Missions
[p] One tiny sensory dare — the South = Sense step of a Compass Run,
              surfaced on its own.
[em] "Find the oldest thing near you and smell
              it. What does age smell like here?"
[em] "Count every blue
              thing you can see without moving your feet."
[p] Done in under
              three minutes; keep one proof line.
[h3] Illuminated Photos
[p] Choose a photo from your day — or let Penny find one — and the
              Book illuminates it, noticing the objects, light, mood, and small
              jokes already inside, then lettering it like a manuscript page you
              can keep.
[figcaption] An ordinary photo of Ron the cat, illuminated.
[h3] Inner Weather
[p] How the day actually felt, in your own words and rendered as
              weather — so a hard afternoon reads as a passing storm and a good
              one as clear skies. The smallest honest check-in there is.
[h3] One-Sentence Souvenir
[p] The whole day, distilled into a single line worth keeping —
              a color, a sound, a tiny mercy — caught before your brain files it
              under ordinary. The smallest page in the deck.
[h3] Cast an Enchantment
[p] Take a photo, pick one of 14 spells, and the Book transforms both
              the image and its main subject.
[em] Everything Speaks
[p] lets the
              object answer in its own voice;
[em] Everything's a Haiku
[p] distills it to three lines;
[em] Mirror, Mirror
[p] reads a selfie
              for insight;
[em] Everything's Roasted
[p] aims a comic burn. Keep
              the illuminated result.
[h3] Gossip
[p] What moved while you were elsewhere. The Academy's cast keep
              scheming, falling out, and warming to one another off-page — and
              this is the word that reaches you about it.
[h3] Letters
[p] A character who has been watching what you keep writes to you
              through the margins — a researched, hand-written note that answers
              something from your real days, addressed to you by name.
[h3] A Fae Bargain
[p] One of the Book Fae gave you something first — and now a small
              sensory return is owed. Pay it in the real world and the Book shifts
              in your favor; leave it, and the debt quietly waits.
[h3] Today's Sky
[p] The Book reads the night actually overhead — the Moon's real phase
              and sign, the Sun's sign, and the nearest reason to look up tonight —
              and keeps it as a page.
[h3] The Bleed
[p] Penny Blackletter's pocket newspaper, where the outside world leaks
              into the margins — odd headlines, an almanac, and an interest column
              that genuinely reaches out to the live web for what's stirring today.
[p] …and dozens more — Diary, Lore, Story Pages,
          Cast Members, Outer Stacks rooms, Classes & Clubs, the Book of You,
          and the rest of the deck, all in the base game.

[h3] Belief
[p] A gentle economy, not a score. You can give or take
[button] Belief
[p] from
              anything in the Book — a character, an object, a recurring motif, even
              a single page. Cast members can fight over those Pages too, warming
              what they love or cooling what they distrust. What you believe in
[em] glows
[p] : the more Belief a thing holds, the more often it
              appears, writes to you, and shapes what the Book surfaces next. It's
              how everyone tells the Book what matters.
[h3] Narrative Weight
[p] Some things earn their place by happening, not by being chosen. Every
              person, place, and motif quietly accrues weight as it shows up in your
              days, your kept pages, and the stories the Book braids. Heavier things
              loom larger and return more readily — so your world naturally centers
              on what you actually keep living with.

[h4] The Binding has noticed you.
[p] Whichever talisman holds the most Belief becomes ascendant. You can
              give Belief to a talisman or try to take it away; so can every member
              of the Cast. Everyone is fighting for what's real in the Book. That
              Chapter colors both the game and the narrative: what scenes surface,
              how characters act, what kind of choices glow, and the tone the
              Labyrinth takes when it writes back.
[p] You do not pick a Chapter from a quiz. Eventually the Binding reads
              the evidence: the Pages you kept, the Belief you gave, and the small
              things you preserved. Then it recognizes where you belong in a
              surprising one-time page of its own.
