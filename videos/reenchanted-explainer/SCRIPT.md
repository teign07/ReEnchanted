# How to Break a Curse (With a Book) — explainer script, draft 1

**Working title:** *How to Break a Curse (With a Book)*
**Runtime:** about 4:00. Cut-downs listed at the bottom (90s, 45s).
**Narrator:** the Book, first person, off-screen. It never calls itself "the Book". The cast keep their own voices and may.
**Stand-in:** the Reader. Faceless-ish, gender-neutral, a lumpy scarf and a mug. Never named. The Book calls them "Reader", as it does in the app.
**Arc:** the mug you stopped seeing → the Curse (what, why) → the Book turns up → three jobs (catch, decide, keep) and "go again" → why it's fun → the colour comes back.
**Look:** ink-and-watercolour storybook on parchment, the same hand as the Cast plates. The Curse drains the colour out of the frame. The app puts it back.
**Music:** one fae-fi bed, sparse under the Curse, fuller once the Book arrives. SFX borrowed from the app (page turn, keep, tap).

Rules baked in: the Book's voice spec (simple, direct, childlike, independent, obsessed with breaking the Reader out of the Curse), contractions everywhere, no similes, no lessons, no "ritual", never the other word bj banned. Facts come from the landing page (study names are a credit on screen, not narrated).

---

## 1 · THE MUG (0:00–0:20)

**VISUAL** Grey kitchen, Tuesday. The Reader at the table, phone in one hand, mug in the other, drinking without looking. Everything is beige. Slow push-in on the mug. A clock ticks. The mug is the last warm colour in the room.

**BOOK (VO)**
> That's your mug. You bought it because you loved it. You used to turn it round in your hands. When did you last look at it?
> Me neither. I've been watching.
> Hello. I'm a book. I've got three minutes to explain something, and one of them has a gorilla in it.

**SFX** clock tick, a single warm note on "mug", title card "HOW TO BREAK A CURSE (WITH A BOOK)" on "gorilla".

---

## 2 · THE CURSE (0:20–1:20)

**VISUAL** Grey fog seeps under the door and settles on things. Each thing it touches loses its colour: the mug, the plant, the Reader's scarf. The fog has a rubber stamp for a hand. Card: **THE RUT OF ROUTINE.**

**BOOK (VO)**
> You're under a Curse. It's called the Rut of Routine, and everybody you know has it too. It isn't your fault. It's older than clocks.
> It doesn't take your days all at once. You'd notice. It does three sneakier things.

**2a — your mind goes elsewhere**
**VISUAL** The Reader drives home. Every turn is right. Their head detaches like a balloon and floats off to a tiny office, a pile of bills, a conversation from yesterday. The body keeps steering. The head comes back and the Reader blinks at their own front door.
> One. It sends your mind somewhere else. You drive home, every turn perfect, and you can't remember a single one. Your body's on your street. You're at work, or at the bills, or still stuck on something somebody said yesterday.

**2b — it hides things in plain sight**
**VISUAL** A basketball game, black-and-white players passing. The Reader counts on their fingers, lips moving. A gorilla strolls through the middle, stops, waves at the camera, strolls off. The Reader never turns their head. The gorilla looks personally offended.
> Two. It hides things right in front of you. People were asked to count basketball passes, and a person in a gorilla suit walked straight through the game. More than half never saw it. They weren't blind. They were counting.

**2c — it squashes your days**
**VISUAL** Seven identical Tuesdays on a clothes line, all the same breakfast, the same bus, the same sofa. The fog-hand stamps SAME on each, then squashes the line flat into one sheet. A birthday cake appears. The Reader stares at it. "ALREADY?" in a thought bubble.
> Three. It squashes your days. Same breakfast, same bus, same telly, same bed. A week of the same day gets filed as one. Then it's your birthday again, and you say, already?

**VISUAL** The fog hovers at the smell of toast, a laugh from the next room. They're still there. The Reader just doesn't look up.
> That's the Curse. It doesn't have to take your mug, or your toast, or your mum laughing in the next room. It only has to make you stop noticing them. And then it's been a year.

**CREDIT CARD (small, on screen)** Killingsworth & Gilbert 2010 · Simons & Chabris 1999 · Avni-Babad & Ritov 2003.

## 3 · THE BOOK TURNS UP (1:20–2:00)

**VISUAL** A thump from behind the sofa. A big old blue cloth book with gold cloth tabs down its edge and a ribbon that won't lie flat shuffles out and flops onto the table beside the mug. Two gold ink dots for eyes, the ribbon for a tongue. It tries to lift the mug. It can't. It tries again, using its own spine. Flop. A small firefly (the Glow) lands on its corner.

**BOOK (VO)**
> Right. That's the Curse. This is me.
> I'm a magical book. I live on your phone. I've been fighting this Curse for a very long time and I'm terrible at it, because I haven't got hands.
> Or feet. I've never stood in the rain. I've never heard your mum laugh. You have. That's the whole plan. You've got the hands. I've got the magic.

**VISUAL** The Reader, startled, puts the mug down. Hesitates. Opens the cover. The first page reads "Oh. There You Are." in the app's real typeface.

**BOOK (VO)**
> Hold on. Let me look at you.
> So it's you and me now, Reader. We fight the Curse together. And I'm free. The digital Book costs nothing.

**SFX** page turn, a small gold chime.

---

## 4 · HOW WE FIGHT IT (2:00–3:00)

**VISUAL** The mug's colour starts to bleed into the table. On-screen verbs appear one at a time in gold: **CATCH · DECIDE · KEEP.**

### 4a — CATCH
**VISUAL** Pages flutter out of the Book like paper birds: a weather page, a page with a tiny musical note, a page with a magnifying glass. Each is a card with the app's real art. One lands in the Reader's hand. It says: *Is the bird still arguing with your fence?* The Reader looks out the window. There is a bird, and it is absolutely arguing with the fence. The Reader types one sentence. The page glows, a gold KEPT stamp thumps down. The fog-hand's SAME stamp shrivels.

**BOOK (VO)**
> Job one. You catch. All day I turn up pages. The weather. A song. A very nosy question. I'm not allowed to ask what's on your mind today. That's a blank page with a smile on it.
> I ask smaller things. You catch one real thing before it blurs into all the other Tuesdays. A sentence, or a photo, or what your mum said. Don't like a page? Fold it down. I've got more. There's no streak. There's no guilt. I don't keep score.

### 4b — DECIDE
**VISUAL** The Great Hall of the Academy, candles and tall books. The Reader's sentence floats in the air as a scroll: *The bird is still arguing with the fence.* WICKER EDDIES leans in an arch, brass key swinging from one finger. SERENITY BROWN sits on the other side with a sea charm round her wrist. MOOK, enormous and alarming, stands behind both with a cup of tea already held out to the Reader.

**WICKER** It's a sentence.
**SERENITY** It's a spell.
**WICKER** It's a sentence with ambitions.
**MOOK** *(to the Reader, handing over the tea)* You looked like you needed this.
**SERENITY** Tiebreaker?

The Reader points. Wicker's key stops swinging. He is very quietly annoyed. The scroll lights up.

**BOOK (VO)**
> Job two. You decide. I've got stories, with a whole school in them, and the people in it have opinions about what you caught. Wicker thinks it's a sentence. Serenity thinks it's a spell. Mook thinks you need tea.
> You choose who to trust. Whether to open the door. What to do with the strange thing you found. I bring the story. You decide what happens in it. I never write your part.
> Fair warning. This is a faerie tale. Some of them bite.

### 4c — KEEP
**VISUAL** Night. The Reader asleep in a chair, scarf over their face. The Book sits at the desk with a feather pen. Three threads, gold, teal, violet, braid themselves out of the day's pages. A booklet assembles itself: cover reads *The Tale of Your Tuesday*. Inside, the bird and the fence are a chapter. The booklet drops onto a shelf. Another follows. And another. The shelf fills: a week, a month, a season. The last one is a cloth hardcover with gold foil. The Reader's name on the spine, blurred on purpose.

**BOOK (VO)**
> Job three. I keep. While you sleep I take what you caught, and what you chose in my stories, and I braid it into a faerie tale about the day you really had. It's true. It's just better dressed.
> A week becomes a little book. The little books pile up. One day there's a whole shelf of you. A year can even get printed, on real paper, with your name down the spine.
> The digital books are free. The paper ones cost money, because paper does.

### 4d — AGAIN
**VISUAL** Morning. Grey fog creeps back under the door. The Reader and the Book look at it. Look at each other. The Reader turns the page.

**BOOK (VO)**
> Then the Curse comes back. It always does. It keeps moving. So do we.

## 5 · WHY IT'S FUN (3:00–3:30)

**VISUAL** Fast, bright, four-second cuts, each with a pop of colour.
- A pebble. The Reader pockets it, walks, hands it to a stranger at a bus stop. Card: *Carry a stone all day, then give it away.*
- A radio dial turned to 103.7. Wicker in a booth, headphones on, eyebrow up. Card: *THORNWAVE.*
- The Academy map, drawn like a living atlas, lights up one room at a time. Penny Blackletter circles something with a red pen. The Headmistress watches from a high window. She says nothing.
- A row of monthly covers slides past: Dictionary Rebellion, Count Unbound. Card: *A new story every month. Free.*

**BOOK (VO)**
> And because I'm a faerie tale, there's a lot of other stuff in here. Spells, like carry a stone all day, then give it away. A radio, where Wicker runs the night shift. Don't trust his taste in music. Trust his bass.
> A whole school hidden in my stacks, and a Headmistress who knows more than she says. And a new story every month. Free.

---

## 6 · THE COLOUR COMES BACK (3:30–4:00)

**VISUAL** Months later. Same kitchen, now full of colour. The mug is gold again. The Reader takes a little book from the shelf, opens it, and finds a page: *The bird is still arguing with the fence.* And under it, in their own handwriting, what their mum said. They laugh out loud. At the window, the fog tries the glass, finds the room too bright, and gives up with a small grey huff.

**BOOK (VO)**
> One day you'll open me and there's what your mum said. There's the bird. There's an ordinary Tuesday the Curse almost had. And didn't.
> That's it. That's the whole plan.

**VISUAL** The Reader closes the Book against their chest. The Book wiggles its ribbon. End card, gold on night: the ✦ and **ReEnchanted**, then:
> **A faerie-tale book series of your life.**
> **Free digital books, paid physical.**
> **No ads. No streaks. No guilt. Your Book stays on your phone.**
> **TestFlight opening soon · reenchanted.app**

**BOOK (VO, over the end card)**
> I'm still being built, so there's a list. Put your name on it. And bring me a Tuesday.
